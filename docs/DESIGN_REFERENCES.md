# What makes the strongest portfolios work

Companion to DESIGN_CLICHES.md. That file lists what to avoid; this one lists what the best sites do well, as principles. Nothing here is a look to copy.
Method (2026-11-25): 15 standouts from the survey plus well-known design-engineer sites, captured at 0.6 s, 3.6 s and three scroll depths (1440px).

## The sites and the one thing each does best

| Site | What works |
|---|---|
| rauno.me | Huge type running sideways as a collage, with one wild colour shape (a yellow, then an orange disc). Restraint everywhere else. It feels like an object, not a page |
| joshwcomeau.com | Personality: illustration, rich colour, small surprises on hover. Generous, warm, clearly one person's site |
| clementgrellier.fr | Scale contrast: a name set edge to edge above crisp product imagery and tiny text. The name animates into place once |
| lisovskiy.work | A signature object (a floppy disk) as the metaphor, then a dense typographic index of projects with years |
| laplaya.studio | One colour committed across huge type, and the work shown as a wall of real thumbnails within one scroll |
| adrienlamy.fr | Full-bleed single colour plus a drawn character: instantly memorable, says "this person" |
| abrahambankole.dev | Name in two tones at enormous size, then a hard switch to a light section; projects as big titles in a list |
| tanayshah.dev | The page is "instrumented": real charts of his own work rhythm. Data used as content, not as decoration |
| alexnaraghi.com | The work leads: big game art straight after one line of intro, then a full-width colour block for the resume |
| brittanychiang.com | Clarity and finish: every project with an image, a clear hover state, nothing missing. (Heavily cloned, so its layout is off-limits) |
| emilkowal.ski, paco.me | Extreme restraint that works only because the craft is in the interactions and the writing. Without that, it reads as empty. This is why our first mocks looked plain |

## Principles to carry over

1. **One huge thing.** Every strong site has one element at a scale that surprises: the name, a word, an image, an object. Everything else is quiet. Our first mocks had a large name and nothing else at scale.
2. **Commit to a colour in big areas.** A full-bleed field or a big shape, not a thin accent line. Rauno's yellow, La Playa's red, Adrien's red, Alex's green block.
3. **Show the work.** Real screenshots and imagery of projects, big. A portfolio without images of the work feels like a resume.
4. **A signature visual tied to the person.** Floppy disk, character, discs. Ours should come from moving and connecting data between systems, drawn abstractly.
5. **One motion moment you remember.** A name assembling, a shape arriving. Once, on load or on a deliberate action. Never on every section.
6. **Tension in the layout.** Overlaps, full-bleed breaks, a section that changes colour hard. Even rhythm reads as a template.
7. **A voice.** Short, specific lines in plain language. No formulas.
8. **Plain words for a general audience.** The best technical portfolios explain what they make in words a recruiter understands; jargon comes later, for those who look.

## What this changes for us
- Lead with a plain-language line about what he builds. SAP appears once in the hero at most and is explained where it appears (see DESIGN_DIRECTIONS.md, "Copy framing").
- Each direction gets one huge element, one committed colour field, a signature visual drawn from data moving between systems, one motion moment, and real project imagery.
- Still never: terminals, code as decoration, glowing orbs, 3D props, stat counters, intros (DESIGN_CLICHES.md).
